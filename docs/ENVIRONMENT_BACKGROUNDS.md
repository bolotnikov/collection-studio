# Environmental background audit

Visual inspection date: 2026-11-13. All 160 original individual images were screened, followed by enlarged inspection of all 84 environmental or architectural observations. No art was generated and no original pixels, original model classifications, category decisions or historical run snapshots were rewritten.

## Findings

The 84 observations comprise 52 category-4 cards, 30 category-5 narrative cards and two category-3 architectural/surface cases (#25 locker and #38 wall-mounted neon sign). The ordinary-object subset therefore contains 54 cards. This is an inventory of source variations, not a production quota.

| Observation                                           | Ordinary objects (54) | All environmental observations (84) |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------: | ----------------------------------: |
| No independent secondary background objects/groups    |                    42 |                                  52 |
| One or two secondary objects/groups                   |                    12 |                                  30 |
| Several secondary objects/groups                      |                     0 |                                   2 |
| Broad masses with simple surface divisions            |                    16 |                                  16 |
| Local small accents                                   |                    32 |                                  56 |
| Repeated small detail in substantial background areas |                     6 |                                  12 |

Counts refer to visibly separate **secondary** objects or coherent groups. Sky/cloud masses, a ground/water plane, a continuous city skyline or merged forest are recorded as major shapes, not as loose props. They can still contain repeated detail. Parts of the main subject, its contents, functional support and interaction partners are excluded from the secondary count. The dataset preserves the actual lists, so these bands must not be presented as literal counts of every leaf, building or cloud.

The common structure is a large readable subject with broad background fields: open sky; sky/water/ground bands; vegetation framing the edges; or enclosing walls/floor with a limited setting cue. The background is subordinate but is not uniformly smooth or always blurred. Grass, leaves, stones, bubbles, water highlights, boards and masonry divisions occur. Detailed foliage (#2, #4, #51, #55, #76), the city panorama (#143) and concert narratives (#109–110) are explicit counterexamples to a blanket claim that sources have no small detail or background buildings.

## Important distinctions

- **Lighthouse #68:** open sky, a cloud band and sea; no independent background buildings, harbour or vehicles.
- **Fan #41:** warm wall, a window and curtain at the left edge; no furnished-room inventory. **Air conditioner #44:** striped wall and ceiling junction; no secondary objects. Wallpaper belongs to an architectural wall, independently of abstract graphic backdrop geometry.
- **Campfire cook #71:** the pot, frame, fire and logs form the main functional group. The forest is a green rear mass; small stones/grass are local accents. **Tent #76:** framing trunks and forest depth; no scattered camping equipment.
- **Bumper car #32:** category 3, material floor with graphic rings behind. It is not an environmental rear backdrop. **Camper #123:** also a patterned backdrop, despite grass under the main object.
- **Claw machine #36:** room corner, floor and walls; the toys are contents of the main machine. **Window #46:** the window/curtain is the main subject; brick wall is background. **Books #22:** the window is instead a background cue.
- **Football #13 and beach ball #96:** the supporting field/sand continues through the image; no separate rear sky is needed. Existing category and backdrop labels remain distinct from environmental detail.

## Classification and production

The hash-bound review is stored in `resources/environment-backdrops.json`, including an explicit in/out screening decision for every original ID and SHA-256. Four optional facet dimensions are available in the database filters and editor: `environmentKind`, `backgroundLayout`, `backgroundDetail`, `backgroundProps`. The accompanying `environment` observation records `majorShapes`, `secondaryObjects`, `smallDetails` and spatial `layout`. These are source observations, not new hard category quotas or artistic scores.

Working knowledge applies these additions by exact original ID/hash before user edits. User edits take precedence. The effective revision includes the review data; new production snapshots freeze the augmented observations. Existing model-analysis keys and the shipped classification-input hash stay unchanged, so this addition requires no recataloguing calls. Historical run profiles are never enriched on read. Imports and explicit database reanalysis receive the new observation instructions in `resources/prompts/knowledge-editor.json`.

The canonical Russian generation policy is the `environment-background` section of `resources/production-requirements.json`. It propagates through planning and generation. The subject design must explicitly describe the major planes and an exhaustive list of independent rear objects. The default is no extra props; a necessary setting cue can use one or two large subordinate objects/groups. This is an authored rule for new images, not an inferred universal source limit. A rocket implies sky and simple support/low horizon; it does not imply additional launchpads, hangars or a miniature spaceport.

Planning and generation input manifests attach background observations to the actual numbered original input. Apply its structural principle without copying its subject or setting inventory. The reference selector also receives the augmented observations. The optional composition review uses the snapshotted policy and records concrete unplanned objects and their positions. Auto-correction requires explicit `wrong-background` evidence plus a contradictory composition observation under the new review protocol; vague detail preferences cannot spend a correction request. Existing per-card/set limits, interruption, image budgets and preservation of all originals remain unchanged. A zero per-card correction limit still skips verification entirely. Historical prompt catalogs without the additive review template retain their old behavior.

## Reproduction and limits

Run `npx tsx scripts/audit-environment.ts`. This verifies all 160 original image hashes and review coverage, then writes enlarged contact sheets and the complete per-card report to `output/environment-audit/`. It performs no provider calls. The checked-in observations were made by visual inspection; counts of coherent groups are editorial judgements, not pixel segmentation measurements.

Offline tests cover complete identity/coverage, source exceptions, persisted user edits, absence of recataloguing, numbered input order, canonical policy propagation, historical/disabled review behavior and evidence-backed correction eligibility. Typecheck, lint, the behavioral suite and build verify implementation. These checks do not measure the artistic results of future image generation.
